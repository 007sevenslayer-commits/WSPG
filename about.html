Who We Are

Women Security Pressure Group (WSPG) is a pioneering, non-governmental umbrella organization established in Nepal in 1991 by women rights activists to eliminate all forms of gender-based violence, discrimination, and exploitation, and ensure equal female participation across all levels of governance, policy-making, and state decision-making.

Established with a non-partisan vision, Women Security Pressure Group (WSPG) serves as a unified force advocating for equal rights, safety, and policy reform across Nepal.

Vision & Mission

Our Vision

Women Security Pressure Group (WSPG) envisions an inclusive, democratic, and just society where every woman enjoys complete autonomy, safety, and equal opportunity across all spheres of life. By uniting diverse political ideologies and non-governmental organizations under a unified, non-partisan umbrella, we strive to build nationwide solidarity that dismantles deep-rooted patriarchal norms, protects marginalized communities, and secures equal partnership for women across all economic, political, and decision-making structures in Nepal. Since our establishment in 1991, we have firmly maintained that an inclusive state is fundamental to national stability, and that investing in women creates a positive feedback loop that uplifts the entire country. Ultimately, our vision is to foster an environment rooted in empathy, ownership, democracy, and peace, where educated and self-reliant women can freely contribute to sustainable socio-economic development without systemic barriers.

Our Mission

Our mission is to lead a relentless movement against gender-based violence, discrimination, and socio-economic oppression through institutional advocacy, legislative lobbying, and grassroots mobilization. Driven by a national network of district branches and civil society partnerships, WSPG holds authorities accountable to reform discriminatory laws, enforce international human rights conventions, strengthen female leadership capacity, and guarantee equal representation for women at every level of governance. We actively work to sensitize the government and civil society on critical social issues—including child marriage, polygamy, and public sexual harassment—ensuring that the struggles of rural and marginalized women are elevated to national platforms. Furthermore, we remain committed to promoting educational equity and vocational empowerment, organizing public campaigns, research initiatives, and capacity-building workshops to equip women with the agency and solidarity required to demand their rightful place in society.

Our Impact

Major Accomplishments & Key Achievements

Legal Policy & Bylaws

WSPG successfully pressured policy makers to make bylaws to the Domestic Violence against Women (DVAW) Act. This led to the introduction of a service centre for victims of domestic violence where women are offered full support.

Constructive Dialogue

Through constructive dialogue conducted by WSPG, policy makers, political leaders, CA members/parliamentarians, judiciary, and security agencies enhanced their understanding to support formulating effective laws as well as supporting law implementation to curb GBV.

Young Women Leaders (YWL) Initiative

WSPG produced 1 Young Women Leader (YWL) to carry forward the initiative to end sexual violence on public transportation in Kathmandu. Through trainings on Patriarchy, Feminism, Power, and Leadership, she alongside WSPG empowered 10 community YWLs—who in turn are preparing a cadre of 100 young women leaders across their respective communities.
